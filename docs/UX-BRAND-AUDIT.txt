EXECUTIVE SUMMARY Video Explanation: https://somup.com/cO1tr7VnXwy The	core	tension	to	resolve	first	Every recommendation in this report flows from one open question: who is sportpharm.com actually for? Today, the site tries to be a B2B clinical resource for athletic trainers and team physicians, and a DTC storefront for everyday consumers, on the same pages, with the same navigation, often in the same scroll. Neither audience gets a clear, fast answer to “what do I do here?” This isn't a cosmetic problem. It's the single biggest drag on conversion across the entire site, and it shows up on nearly every page audited below: unclear CTAs, services explained in clinical terms instead of athlete outcomes, and a homepage that — at 100% zoom — never tells a first-time visitor what SportPharm does or what to click next. The	recommended	fix:	route	by	visitor	intent,	don't	force	one	homepage	to	do	three	jobs	Add an entry experience that asks the visitor who they are, then routes them to a page built for that goal: • Professional Athlete / Team — routes to the B2B/B2P experience: compounding, concierge pharmacy, team bundles, conference-driven credibility. • Everyday Athlete — routes to a DTC experience built around problems and outcomes (recovery, soreness, travel kits), not clinical service names. • Sports Medicine Professional — routes to the clinical/partnership experience: drug repackaging, supplement support, retail pharmacy services. This single change resolves most of the friction documented below without requiring a full rebuild — it lets the site keep its B2B depth and its DTC ambitions without asking one page to speak to both at once.   OPEN QUESTION FOR LEADERSHIP What is the actual goal of the website — and of the brand overall? Right now there's a live tension between protecting the pro-sports/B2B identity and the ambition to get WasabiRub into everyday people's hands. The intent-routing fix works either way, but the content, tone, and growth investment behind each path should follow a clear answer to this question, not the reverse.

Services	Video	Overview:	https://somup.com/cO1t32VnXwS	The Services section covers five distinct offerings. Each is reviewed individually below, but one pattern repeats across most of them and is worth fixing once, site-wide, rather than five separate times: REPEATED ISSUE ACROSS SERVICES PAGES “Service Inquiry” is used as the call-to-action on multiple pages, but it reads like a car-dealership lead form — it doesn't tell the visitor what will actually happen next. Compounding	ISSUES RECOMMENDATIONS – There's a missed opportunity for imagery or real examples of what's been compounded for athletes in the past — the page talks about the service in the abstract rather than grounding it in athlete outcomes. – Padding between sections is too large, causing visitors to lose interest before they finish scrolling to learn more.  
– Reframe around the athlete directly: “Here's how we help athletes like you.” Add real examples or representative use cases of compounds created for athletes. – “Service Inquiry” as a CTA doesn't communicate what the visitor is actually signing up for – Try “Start a Compounding Request” or something equally specific  – Move the testimonial up, near the top of the page, so credibility is established before visitors have to scroll for it. – Rework the WasabiRub placement so it feels like a relevant next step for the visitor, not an inserted advertisement. – The compounding categories list (Women's Health, Veterinarian Compounding, Dental Products, etc.) is comprehensive but generic/clinical — since this page's strength is the athlete angle, consider leading with the 3–4 categories most relevant to athletes before the full list.

ISSUES RECOMMENDATIONS – The page doesn't say what kinds of supplements are actually available through SportPharm — there are no examples or links to give the offer shape. – Most visitors don't arrive already knowing which supplement they want — they know the problem they're trying to solve in their body. – Add specific examples or links to supplement options. A sugar-free recovery drink mix could be a strong, concrete addition here.  – Organize the page around problems and outcomes (“for recovery,” “for joint support,” “for energy”) rather than supplement categories, so visitors can find their way in by symptom, not by product knowledge they may not have. – Category list in the FAQ (Hydration & Electrolyte Support, Muscle & Joint Recovery, etc.) is genuinely useful but is buried at the very bottom of an FAQ accordion — this is exactly the kind of “what do you actually offer” content that should be visible without opening an FAQ.  Idea worth exploring: partner with an at-home blood test provider (e.g., a company like Rythm) and build supplement support recommendations tied to specific test results. This mirrors what's working for direct-to-consumer personalized supplement brands (Vitamin Lab, Nourished, and similar), which people respond to strongly — individually labeled packets tailored to them. The underlying theme: people want SportPharm to solve the problem for them. Right now, this page puts most of the work of figuring out what to buy on the visitor.  Drug	Repackaging	No changes recommended. This is a specific, well-defined service that visitors search for directly — anyone landing on this page already knows they want drug repackaging. Retail	Pharmacy	ISSUES RECOMMENDATIONS – The opening copy block reads negatively, almost apologetic in tone — when the underlying service is genuinely valuable. – Rewrite the opening block to lead with the value of the service rather than a downbeat framing.
