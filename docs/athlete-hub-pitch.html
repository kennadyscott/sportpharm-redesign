Where the hub should live

Two defensible paths. The trade is speed versus brand equity — a business call, not a technical one.

Option A

wasabirub.com

sportpharm.com stays the company site. The hub and the product live together, so everything the content earns compounds into the WasabiRub brand.

Gain: traffic lands where the purchase happens, and every ranking, backlink and share builds the product brand itself.

Cost: a new domain starts at zero search authority. Expect 6–12 months to meaningful organic traffic.

Option B

sportpharm.com/athlete-hub

The hub sits on the established domain and drives hard to wasabirub.com from every page.

Gain: inherits years of existing domain authority — the single biggest accelerant available. Ranks materially sooner.

Cost: traffic lands on SportPharm first, adding a step before purchase, and the equity builds under SportPharm rather than WasabiRub.

The real question is not which ranks faster — it is what we are building. Option A trades speed for brand equity in WasabiRub. Option B trades brand concentration for reach sooner. Both are defensible; they answer different questions.

Worth saying plainly

Health content is held to a higher standard by search engines than almost any other category, and we would be building from zero on a domain that sells the product. Anyone promising fast rankings here is guessing. This is a compounding investment, not a campaign — but unlike advertising, it does not stop the day we stop paying.

Two stores, same three products

The hub, the products and the cart all live on wasabirub.com — so nothing is lost in transit. The problem is on the other side of the split.

sportpharm.com is WordPress running WooCommerce. It sells WasabiRub, IcetraRub and Super Hot today. wasabirub.com will sell the same three products through Payload + Stripe. Same catalogue, two systems that do not know about each other.

This is the real version of the concern raised in the room: not a cart that empties in transit, but two carts that never see each other. A customer who starts on sportpharm.com and finishes on wasabirub.com has a half-filled basket on a site they have left — and we would be reconciling two order streams, two stock counts and two discount systems for one product line.

Recommendation: wasabirub.com becomes the only store. sportpharm.com stops selling and becomes what it is best at — the company, the pharmacy, the brick-and-mortar presence — with a Store item in its menu that deep-links to wasabirub.com. One catalogue, one checkout, one order stream, one place a customer's history lives.

That is already the direction in the site notes. It needs saying out loud on Tuesday, because it means retiring WooCommerce as a selling system — a decision with an owner and a migration attached, not a toggle.

One technical caution

Stripe payment links and a cart are not the same thing. Payment Links check out one product at a time and cannot do a multi-item basket, order-level promo codes, or free-shipping-over-$75 — all of which the prototype cart had. A real cart means Stripe Checkout with line items assembled by the Payload backend. Well-trodden, and the chosen stack supports it — but back-end work, not a link pasted onto a product page. Worth being precise about before it is scoped as the easy part.

Status today

None of it is wired. There is no Stripe code on the live site and no working cart — the prototype went away with the WasabiRub layout revert. Every Add to Cart button currently on the site shows a toast and does nothing.

What it actually requires

Ongoing, not one-time. This is the part that decides whether it works.

  • Clinical review of every article. Pharmacist and sports-medicine sign-off, with named authors and reviewers published on the page. This is an ethical obligation and a ranking requirement — search engines explicitly weight author credentials for health content.
  • A publishing cadence. A hub that stops updating stops ranking. This needs an owner, not a launch date.
  • Editorial discipline. The moment articles become disguised product pitches, they stop ranking and stop earning trust.

Already built and paid for: the hub pages, both interactive tools, the CMS and review workflow, and six drafted articles.
